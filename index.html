The movement of a priestly family

Origin

Mulvir, Pernem

The remembered beginning of this lineage is priestly service at Mulvir temple in Pernem, Goa.

Pernem, also known as Pedne, sits at Goa's northern edge near Maharashtra, a region shaped by temple villages, border movement and old Konkani-Marathi cultural exchange.

Patronage

Kowad

A later turning point came when ancestors of Marathi writer Ranjit Desai are remembered to have brought Hari Bhat or his descendants to Kowad for regular pooja at the Desai residence.

Kowad is in Chandgad taluka of Kolhapur district, close to Belagavi.

Migration

Three Directions

After several generations in Kowad, the family story spreads into three broad geographies: Belagavi, Bangalore and northern Karnataka.

Bangalore relatives became associated with Karada Brahmin Sangha networks through Marathi language and community memory.

Bangalore

Babu Bhat on Surveyor Street

Babu Bhat, also remembered as Bapu Bhat, was a son of Purushottam Bhat. Family history identifies him as a government surveyor and resident of Surveyor Street in Bangalore.

A Bangalore Mirror local-history article gives the wider context of Surveyor Street as a place associated with surveyors who came to Bengaluru during city expansion.

Investigation

Narsoba and Dattatreya

Your Kowad investigation suggests a possible ritual confusion: a Narsoba temple in nearby Nittur may have been overlooked, while similarity of names led some ancestors to associate the lineage with Narsoba Wadi and Dattatreya worship.

Dutta Joshi, son of Vasant Joshi from the *1 branch, recalled a family memory that the kuladevata may instead be connected with Yoga Narasimha temple at Nittur, Maharashtra.

Your branch

Sitaram > Balakrishna > Govind Rao > Umashankar > Subramanya

About the Panashikar Name

The handwritten family tree uses the label Joshi [Panashikar] Kovadkar, but family evidence does not suggest a connection to the Panashikar sweet-maker or sweet-seller tradition, similar to the Halwai occupation.

In the remembered history of this line, the known occupations are temple priests, surveyors and similar government roles. No known ancestor from this branch is remembered as being in the sweet-making or sweet-selling business.

Mulvir Region

Pernem/Pedne is Goa's northern gateway, bordered by Maharashtra and connected by river, road and coastal movement. In family memory, this is the sacred starting landscape.

Kowad Region

Kowad sits in Chandgad taluka, Kolhapur district, near Belagavi. Its borderland position helps explain family movement toward Karnataka towns while keeping ties to Kowad temples and village memory.

Karada Brahmin Culture

Karhade/Karada Brahmins are associated with Marathi and Konkani-speaking regions of Maharashtra, Goa and Karnataka. For the Bangalore side, Marathi language appears to have helped preserve community identity after migration.

Family Deity

Recent family belief holds Kolhapur Mahalakshmi and Dattatreya as kuladevata, but this remains contested in the family memory. One concern is that Dattatreya may not fit the older kuladevata pattern remembered by some family members, where Shiva-Parvati or Vishnu-Lakshmi forms are more commonly expected.

Mahalakshmi could represent the female deity, but the missing male deity has created confusion. In your investigation, Dutta Joshi, son of Vasant Joshi from the *1 branch, concluded that his own kuladevata practice has shifted to Mulvir Shiva temple and Bhagavati temple in Mulvir. He also recalled his mother's belief that the family kuladevata could be connected to Yoga Narasimha temple in Nittur, Maharashtra.

Gotra and Vedic lineage

Bharadwaja Gotra, Rigvedic Tradition

आङ्गिरस-बार्हस्पत्य-भारद्वाज-त्रिप्रवरान्वितः ऋग्वेदान्तर्गत-आश्वलायनसूत्र-शाकलशाखाध्यायी भारद्वाजगोत्रः

गोत्रोत्पन्नोऽहं सुब्रह्मण्यशर्मा अभिवादये ॥

This is an abhivadanam-style lineage statement. It identifies the person as belonging to Bharadwaja gotra, with the three pravaras Angirasa, Barhaspatya and Bharadwaja. It also states a Rigvedic affiliation through the Ashwalayana sutra and Shakala shakha.

In family interpretation, this supports the conclusion that the lineage followed a Rigvedic tradition. That is meaningful because Rigvedic practice is remembered as common in the Mulvir-Goa region and also creates a shared denominator with Karhade/Karada Brahmin identity.

Pernem temple landscape

Mulvir and Bhagavati Temples

The family's oldest remembered sacred geography is in Pernem, Goa. Hari Bhat and his ancestors are remembered as priests connected with Mulvir temple, and Dutta Joshi from the *1 branch continues to treat Mulvir Shiva temple and Bhagavati temple in Mulvir as the kuladevata practice.

These two temples help explain why the family story should not be reduced only to the later Dattatreya or Kolhapur Mahalakshmi belief. Mulvir and Bhagavati preserve the Pernem-side memory of worship, priestly duty and village deity tradition.

Kuladevata investigation

Nittur Narasimha Temple

Nittur Narasimha Temple, also referred to in family discussion as Yoga Narasimha or Narsoba temple, is important because it may explain one branch of the family's kuladevata confusion. Dutta Joshi, son of Vasant Joshi from the *1 branch, recalled that his mother believed the family deity could be connected with this temple in Nittur, Maharashtra.

This does not settle the question for the whole family. It records a living investigation: current belief includes Kolhapur Mahalakshmi and Dattatreya, Dutta Joshi continues to follow Mulvir Shiva temple and Bhagavati temple in Mulvir, and Nittur Narasimha remains a meaningful candidate remembered through his mother's account.

The renovation board in the images identifies the issuing group as Shri Narsinha Devalaya Jirnoddhar Mandal and Villagers, Nittur, meaning the Shri Narsinha Temple Renovation Committee and villagers of Nittur.
